This morning a small private steamer arrived here from Sretensk, which brought, among others, a young German merchant who is to enter the firm of Langelütge in Vladivostok. In addition, at noon today during dinner at Mr. Kloss’s in the firm of Kunst & Albers, I made the acquaintance of Mr. Albers Jr., the son of one of the heads of the firm. *Tres faciunt collegium*, and so in the evening we find ourselves together again in the dining room of the Grand Hotel “Mon géni”. Several other Germans join us. Soon we also discover a room in which a “Blüthner” stands, and over a bowl of punch we celebrate a thoroughly pleasant German evening. The young merchant, who left home only five weeks ago, is a jolly fellow; he has brought along Berlin couplets still unknown here, and so “Die lauschige Nacht” and “Auf dem Baume, da hängt ’ne Pflaume” ring out for the first time in innermost Siberia, in the capital of the Priamurskaya.

August 2.

It is, of course, exactly as Mr. Kloss predicted: the expected mail steamer has not arrived, the agent does not know at all where it currently is, but he “hopes” that it will arrive tomorrow. Nikolai Mikhailovich has already visited all the shipowners of Blagoveshchensk and ascertained that a private passenger steamer is scheduled to depart tomorrow. If the mail steamer does not arrive, then we may perhaps travel on a private steamer. Throughout the entire day, pompous processions alternate with military parades. At noon a grand parade takes place on the market square, and an illumination in the evening. Today, as already mentioned, is the one-year anniversary of the liberation of the city from the Chinese peril.

August 3.

Having traveled toward the water with the greatest difficulty to get this far, we might really expect some accommodation on the part of the water now. But no such luck; on the contrary, it is falling day by day. The dispatches from the upper course of the Amur report ever lower water levels. Naturally, the mail steamer has not arrived today either. [[p266]] Nikolai Mikhailovich is in possession of a passport from the Tsar’s own hand, which places him on an equal footing with an imperial courier. On the strength of this passport, he energetically demands of the mail line’s agent that he be transported as expeditiously as possible. In view of this, the agent admits that the mail steamer has run aground en route and suffered an accident that must first be makeshiftly repaired, and that it is therefore very questionable when the steamer will arrive. That is the last straw; our entire traveling party declares a boycott against the wretched mail steamer line with its steamers that make no headway and its eternal fish menus. Since the advertised private steamer is also slow in coming, Nikolai Mikhailovich and Ivan Ivanovich set out together on a search. Their combined efforts succeed in ferreting out a very small steamer that draws only two feet of water, three and a half with cargo, and is said never to have run aground even at the lowest water level. With this steamer, we can travel all the way to Sretensk without changing ships. On the condition that the steamer sails tomorrow, they immediately book all the available cabin berths. We shall therefore be able to travel on tomorrow afternoon. They immediately tell me [[p267]] triumphantly that the steamer belongs to a German who will also be traveling along to Sretensk himself. Indeed, the owner bears an entirely German name. He is named Neumann, but is a Russian subject and, of course, speaks not German, but *daitsch*.
