With commendable candour, the proprietor of the Foreign Hotel told me various details. Thus it happened that Cossacks—Russians are most often referred to as the thieving rats—turned up offering an ox, on another occasion 500 sacks of flour at 50 cents a sack, then again a crate of beer, a crate of champagne; indeed, whole junk-loads were sold. But the thieves did not spare their buyers either; they broke into their premises [[p28]] themselves again by night and robbed and plundered whatever they could find. The next day they would go to the competitor and dispose of the goods there. Sometimes it happened that goods were stolen from one man, which the other bought, only to have them stolen from him in turn and found the next day with the first owner. At that time, a kind of communism seems to have prevailed among the shopkeepers in Tongku, or at least among the vagabond adventurers—and there were quite a number of them. In the possession of one of these “merchants,” a substantial lot of preserved fruit was found that had been destined for the German field hospitals and stolen. He met with his well-deserved punishment: two years’ imprisonment and a fine of 3,000 dollars.

By way of illustration, another delightful story that the talkative innkeeper tells me in an indescribably comical mixture of Austrian and Jewish jargon: “So I bought an ox from a Cossack—for twenty dollars. Max comes to me, I show him the ox, says he, that’s my ox, they stole it from me in the night. Say I to Max: ‘Max,’ say I, ‘the ox cost me twenty dollars what I gave the Cossack. We’re both businessmen; good, I’ll give you back the ox if you give me back my twenty dollars.’ ‘Good,’ says Max, says he, ‘I’ll give you twenty dollars.’ He gives me twenty dollars, takes the ox, and walks off. I go get the Cossack, say to him: ‘You dog, you stole the ox what you sold me for twenty dollars from Max! I had to give back the ox to Max, you give me back the twenty dollars.’ Max had his ox back, I myself had back my twenty dollars from Max, plus twenty dollars from the Cossack. I made twenty dollars pure profit.”

That is how business is done in Tongku.
