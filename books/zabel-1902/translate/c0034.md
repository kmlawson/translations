The opportunity to present requests and complaints must be seized, and there is much to complain about. A few days ago, marauding Cossacks attempted to break into the English consulate. The Chinese watchman barred their entry and closed the gate. At that, one of the Cossacks raised his rifle and shot the watchman in the abdomen through the door. The Cossacks escaped. For as long as I have been in Newchwang, no one has been able to track the fellows down. Rumours were indeed circulating that the murderer had been found and executed by firing squad under martial law, but in reality this is not the case; the investigation that was launched did lead to a trail, but to no punishment.

Another complaint is brought forward by the head of an English firm based in Newchwang. He wished to bring up a substantial sum of silver to Newchwang for the branch of the Yokohama Specie Bank, but the Russians in Port Arthur are preventing him from using the railway, on which everyone else travels free of charge.

The German consul, too, should have used the opportunity to lodge a complaint. The German hotel in which I am staying was attacked one night. The intruders were tampering with the gate and threatening the Chinese watchman. Awakened by the commotion, the hotelkeeper and I ultimately had to rush downstairs, revolver and Winchester in hand, to investigate the cause of the racket, only to find that it was not the Chinese bandits—who carry on their mischief every night in the Chinese city—who had paid us a visit, but our protectors, the Cossacks. The next day the hotelkeeper went to the German consul to request vigorous protection against a recurrence of such assaults; the consul, however, took no steps to comply with this request.

[[p75]]

And so the audience proceeds: first come the official representatives of the foreign states, then the directors of the Russo-Chinese Bank, and finally the rest *secundum ordinem*. I personally am received together with the head of the firm Bush Brothers, the leading English house in town, and the director of the Yokohama Specie Bank. His Excellency receives us standing, graciously offers his hand, and is of a truly excellent amiability. Alekseyev is of stocky build and possesses a stately *embonpoint*; above the round curve of his cheekbones flash a pair of friendly yet cunning eyes; his lightly tanned, intelligent face, with its slightly curved, prominent nose, is framed by a full black beard parted in the middle. Such is roughly the appearance of the future Russian Minister of the Navy.

First, after inviting us all to be seated, he greets me, specifically asking whether I speak French. Perhaps he does not know quite what I want of him, and he probably suspects, quite rightly, that the others will not understand French. The conversation is therefore conducted in French. I begin by reminding him that I had already been presented to him at Beitang on the day of the assault, speak briefly of my journey from Peking to Newchwang, and finally explain to him that I intend to travel through Manchuria via Mukden, Jilin, Harbin, etc., to Vladivostok, but that given the present insecurity in the country I cannot risk making the journey without assuring myself of his support and protection. He asks me this and that about the execution of my plan and ultimately declares to me [[p76]] with positively charming amiability that he would grant me every facility within his power for this journey.
