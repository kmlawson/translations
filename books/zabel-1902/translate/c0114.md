Then come meringues. In addition, a large bowl of confectionery stands on the table, or rather it circulates without ceasing. Between eating and drinking one nibbles on sweets, the famous Russian confections with all the trimmings! Between eating and drinking, however, one also smokes cigarettes vigorously! The long cigarette holder of the gentleman with the full white beard is back as well. One cannot eat and drink as fast as the courses succeed one another. From the right comes punch again, or something of the sort. "Allow me to prepare that for you?" The General takes my only half-full glass away. I watch him intently as he brews the punch. First he puts a piece of fresh pineapple into the glass, then comes a small spoonful of fruit preserved in sugar, on top of that a good splash of cognac, and finally, topping it all off, champagne. Also genuinely Russian, remarks my kind host. I taste it. Indeed, genuinely Russian; for a German palate accustomed to punch—hm—a matter of taste! Whenever one has taken a drink, the host immediately tops it up again, so that one does not notice how much one is drinking. Nor is one supposed to. When refilling, it makes no difference at all what it is topped up with. Whatever is nearest to hand is taken: champagne, red wine, white wine, and cognac in turn, sometimes one thing from the right and something else simultaneously from the left. The mixture is different with almost every sip. A sip of punch, a piece of confection, a puff of the cigarette, a morsel of white bread with Mongolian cheese, a praline, another puff of the cigarette, a sip of red wine, a spoonful of iced coffee, a puff of the cigarette, a small piece of meringue, a sip of punch, different mixture, another puff of the cigarette—in this manner we amuse ourselves for quite a while. In between, conversation buzzes back and forth; the General’s wife is the most amiable and graceful hostess in the world. Then the gathering is temporarily interrupted; people take a little stroll in the garden, which, to be sure, consists mostly of scrub and lawn. The little daughter is allowed to join. Oh, she can speak German too! "Do speak some German with the gentleman; come, do speak German!" She is shy and will not speak German, even though the governess urges her as well. Instead, two seconds later she clears a wide ditch with a high leap. Meanwhile, the orderlies have placed a large samovar of brightly polished brass on the table. We return to the veranda, and our gracious hostess serves each person tea with her own hands. I am charged with the obligation of drinking tea in the Russian fashion. Do I know how? Yes, certainly! They do not believe it. I am to demonstrate. Everyone watches eagerly to see how I will drink tea. I place a lump of sugar on my tongue and sip the tea over it. Everyone roars with delight and claps their hands: "He already knows how to drink Russian tea!" I always used to think that only we Germans back home behave towards a foreigner as if we had a chimpanzee before us that must be pampered and trained. The Russian, however, is far worse in this respect! For a time it is remarkably pleasant to play the part of the chimpanzee. Meanwhile, however, it has grown late. I was only invited for tea and do not know how long Russian custom permits one to stay. At any rate, it will do no harm if I take my leave now. They insist that I stay longer. I would like to very much, for teatime was truly delightful. But it is better that I make a graceful exit and go while everyone is most sorry to see me leave. Well, then in any case I should like to wait a moment [[p230]] until the coachman has harnessed the horses. Not at all, I shall walk. But no, I must be driven to my hotel. Well then, the gentleman from the Sakhalin Governorate will ride along as well. "Goodbye, many heartfelt thanks, a pleasant journey, do not forget to send us the photographs that we are in!" More handshakes—wait, but do not shake hands across the threshold, that brings bad luck! The elegant troika takes us back to the hotel. Now I am sitting in my room and am still in such a wonderful

20 July.

Khabarovsk possesses a number of sights. The most prominent is the large cathedral built of red brick. Adjoining it at ground level is a low framework with numerous bells that ring harmoniously together when striking the hours. At New Year's, everyone has the right to ring the bells.
