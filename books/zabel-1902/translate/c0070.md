The “Alexander,” on which I embarked, is a steamer, and a rather peculiar one at that. For its engine is located not in the middle of the vessel, but in its final third, counting from the bowsprit. The entire forward part of the vessel is cargo hold. Although the “Alexander” is a freight steamer, by no means a passenger steamer, officials of the company almost always travel on it as passengers, especially when it visits regions where no other steamers go. The passengers are then accommodated in the quarters intended for the captain and the mate. If one wishes to get into the cabin, one must first climb up to the bridge on a ladder with iron rungs, and from there go back down a wooden flight of stairs into the cabin situated beneath the bridge. The stairs lead into the saloon, with the captain’s cabin on the right and the mate’s cabin on the left, all three compartments taken together being about six meters long and three meters [[p150]] wide. When one considers that every human being needs a bunk about two meters long and three-quarters of a meter wide to sleep in, one can easily calculate that five persons of not all too substantial girth can reside here quite comfortably after all. I was assigned a sofa, which unfortunately does not reach the requisite width of three-quarters of a meter that I need for lying down, but measures only about 45 centimeters in width. To keep me from falling off, a board was fixed vertically in front of the open side of the sofa. As a result, I would at least have room lying on my side. When the ship then began to roll underway, I would lie with my back against the wall and my chest against the board placed in front, nicely wedged in, but all the more secure. Yet unfortunately, it was only with my chest that I fit into the berth, for somewhat further down, to my sorrow, my diameter on all sides exceeds the norm of 45 centimeters. So what to do? The remaining space on the ship had been given away. First I try the arrangement, and lo and behold, it works if I lie on my left side. If, on the other hand, I want to turn onto my right side, which one does feel the urge to do now and then, I always have to get up first, move the pillow to the other end of the sofa, and only then can I make myself comfortable again. This shifting of the pillow had to take place because I necessarily had to sleep with my back to the wall; otherwise, too much center of gravity would have hovered over the edge of the sofa. But since I am absolutely not accustomed to sleeping on my side, I cannot fall asleep at all. The only thing about me that falls asleep is my right or my left arm, depending on whether I am lying on my right or my left side. Finally, the saving idea occurs to me: not to set the board up vertically, but to lay it horizontally across two folding chairs, and myself on top of it. That works for a while. I lie half on the board, half on the sofa. Once the ship gets underway, I actually sleep quite splendidly, but have an oppressive dream that troubles me for a long time. Namely, I dream that I am in danger of tumbling down a steep cliff. In the end, there is no holding on; I fall in fact, and from the sudden fright I awake, only to find myself lying on the floor of the cabin. The ship is rolling heavily, and because of this the board on the folding chairs has gradually moved away from my bed, finally sailing down along with me. But I am too tired to make any further arrangements, lie down on my left side again, and brace my right arm against the table bolted to the floor so as not to start rolling again. Thus I sleep until morning. To be sure, my left arm has to be specially woken up again.

The next night, the mate gives up his bunk to me. It is considerably wider and just suffices for me. He himself, however, measures a mere 35 centimeters across the chest at the widest point, and eats a little less for supper, so that he has room on the sofa, even with the vertical board in front, lying on his side, of course. But he claims that he always sleeps on his left side. Well, then it works!
