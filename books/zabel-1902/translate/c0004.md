*) An example of dialect differences: In South China, a river appears on the map as “Pe-kiang.” That is Northern Chinese and means “North River.” On the spot, no one understands if you ask for the “Pe-kiang,” because the same written character is pronounced “Pak-koyk” there. This example also shows that the Mandarin dialect can by no means be described as the Chinese written language taking the place, as it were, of High German, since the characters are simply pronounced quite differently depending on the region. This goes so far that Chinese and Japanese can even communicate with each other by means of Chinese characters, without being able to understand one another if each were to read the characters aloud in their own language.

The illustrations in the present work are for the most part reproductions of my own photographs. From a number of photographs that were unsuitable for direct reproduction because of their lack of sharpness [[p15]] or their inartistic composition, drawings in pen and ink were produced by the painter Mr. C. Arriens in Berlin. Mr. Arriens has been a loyal collaborator, whom I thank above all for his sensitive appreciation of those elements in the illustrations that are characteristic, or ethnologically or geographically significant.

At this point, I warmly thank all those who have kindly lent their assistance to the present work.

Wilmersdorf near Berlin, October 1902.

Rudolf Zabel.
