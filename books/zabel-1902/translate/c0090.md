### 2. Chronology of Siberian History from the Death of Yermak to the Appearance of Muravyov.

1584. At Yermak's death, the boundaries of the Russian acquisitions in Siberia are formed to the northeast by the confluence of the Ob and Irtysh and the small river Narym, to the southeast by the Ishish, a tributary of the Irtysh, and to the south by the Tobol and the Tura.
1586. Founding of Tyumen and Tobolsk.
1590. As an experiment, 30 peasant families from Solvychegodsk are settled beyond the Urals.
1593. First exile of political criminals to Western Siberia.
1600. Establishment of a postal route between Moscow and Transbaikalia. Tsar Fyodor Alekseyevich settles peasant volunteers from the Perm region in Western Siberia on favorable terms, specifically along the rivers Tura, Tavda, Tobol, Irtysh, and Ob and their tributaries.
1600. Founding of Turinsk.
1604. Founding of Tomsk.
1618. Founding of Kuznetsk and Yeniseysk.
1628. Founding of Krasnoyarsk on the Yenisei.
1630. Cossacks reach the Stanovoy Range in the Far East.
1630. 100 men and 150 women and girls are settled in and around Tobolsk.
1632. Yakutsk is founded.
1635. Olyokminsk on the Lena is founded.
1636–1642. Traveling along the Lena, the Cossack Busa reaches the coast of the Arctic Ocean, sails out upon it, and discovers the mouths of the Olenyok, Yana, and Indigirka, as well as a new tribe on their banks, the Yukaghirs.
1640. The Siberian *yasak* yields, among other things, 6,800 sable pelts.
1641. Vasily Vlasyev subjugates the Buryats south of Yakutsk.
1643. Vasily Poyarkov is the first to reach the Amur, and by way of it the Sea of Okhotsk, traveling from Yakutsk along the Lena and then the rivers Aldan, Uchur, and Gonam, then crossing the Stanovoy Range and sailing down the Bryanta and Zeya. He returns overland from the coast to Yakutsk.

1644. Mikhail Stadukhin discovers the Kolyma and founds Nizhnekolymsk near its mouth on the Arctic Ocean. Nearby, he discovers rich deposits of mammoth bones and tusks, the extraction of which becomes a new branch of industry.

1647. Ivan Posichakov collects the yasak from the Buryats inhabiting the regions around Lake Baikal, travels south along the Ingoda, and is the first to reach Mongolia and its capital, Urga. Here he induces Khan Yezen to send an embassy to the Tsar, which departs for Moscow the following year.

1647. Ivan Afanasyev, with 51 Cossacks, defeats more than 1,000 Tungus living between the Sea of Okhotsk and the Stanovoy, discovers the Okhota, and founds the ostrog of Okhotsk on its banks, which faces fluctuating fortunes in constant fighting with the Tungus.

1648. Semyon Dezhnyov is the first to sail around the eastern tip of Asia from the mouth of the Kolyma, discovering the strait later rediscovered by Bering and named after him. He finds the coastal river Anadyr, on whose banks he establishes a *zimovye*. Meanwhile, two other explorers, Motora and Stadukhin, have likewise set out overland from Yakutsk, but arrive at the Kolyma later than Dezhnyov. Stadukhin in particular begrudges him the glory of having been first, robs him of the yasak collected along the way, and drives him off. Further south, Dezhnyov scratches out a living for a long time on the proceeds of seal hunting.

1649–1653. The Yakutsk merchant Khabarov equips an expedition to the Zeya and the lower Amur at his own expense; he drives out the resident Daurs, but comes into serious conflict with the Chinese for the first time in Siberian history. His discoveries are received with great interest in Europe; he himself is summoned there to make a report, and the government equips an expedition under Stepanov, which meets with mixed success yet leads to no permanent results. Stepanov himself falls in 1657 in battle with the Chinese, who also completely wipe out his force.

1652. Founding of Irkutsk.

1654. Founding of Nerchinsk. Pashkov, the voivode of Yeniseysk, undertakes an expedition to Lake Baikal and the land beyond the lake. He reaches the Nercha and founds Nerchinsk on its banks.

1665. Nikifor Chernigovsky, who murdered Voivode Obukhov of Ilimsk and is therefore pursued by the authorities, flees with a number of deportees to the Amur Valley and founds the first Russian colony on the upper reaches of the Amur, Albazin, which subsequently becomes the Russians' strongest stronghold against Chinese attacks. The Kangxi Emperor of China protests against this renewed attempt by the Russians to establish themselves in the Amur Valley. Milovanov is sent to Beijing as the first Russian envoy. Meanwhile, Nikifor Chernigovsky and the other fugitives of Albazin are pardoned; the settlement itself is officially recognized by the voivode of Nerchinsk and brought under administration by Ivan Poltekov in 1674.

1675. Nikolai Spafary is sent as an envoy to Beijing on account of repeated protests from China and is received cordially there. In the following year he is sent there again to demand exclusive navigation rights on the Zeya and the lower Amur, albeit without success.
