We exchange greetings, the gentlefolk take their seats near me, and I cannot rid myself of the feeling that they want something from me. Once the general makes a start as if he were about to address me, then another furtive glance from the general's wife catches me, and in between an evidently very difficult question is being deliberated in Russian, the subject of which—with all modesty I cannot help thinking—must be myself. Finally one of the generals turns to me and asks in German whether I am a German. "Yes." And now at last it comes out what is actually the matter. The general's wife had had little sleep the previous night, and so the couple, together with the white-bearded gentleman, had wanted to secure a compartment all to themselves. The old gentleman, however, was to be accommodated in another compartment for the night so that the general's wife could sleep undisturbed. Wretch that I was, my stepping in had now thwarted this fine plan—hence the incomprehensible "madame," and hence the reproachful looks! And in truth, I would not have known what to do with my first-class ticket either, had I not taken that seat. In first class, virtually every seat was taken. Yet even so, of course, Madame shall have her night's rest as far as I am concerned, not another word need be said about it; the gentleman with the white beard has already scouted out that somewhere on the train, in second class, the uppermost of three folding berths stacked one above the other, directly under the ceiling, is still vacant, and it is reserved for me for the night. They are delighted at my kindness; the general, visibly moved, shakes my hand; the general's wife, just as moved, shakes my hand as well—pretense here, pretense there. My general speaks French, my general's wife a little German and French. The other generals all speak French, some of them German. The conversation is conducted alternately, as the case may be, in one language or the other, sometimes even in both at once. The gentleman with the white beard and the long cigarette holder speaks only Russian and talks away at [[p212]] me. Although I do not understand him, I nod my head vigorously. This pleases him so much that he places all his cigarettes at my disposal. Then the four of us return to our compartment. The general invites me to tea, the general's wife to sweets. We chat about all manner of things: about the region, the Chinese campaign, Khabarovsk, where the general is stationed, Warsaw, where he was previously. The general's wife tells of her little daughter, who in Khabarovsk is looking forward to her parents' return, and must be a little beauty if she takes after her mother. Well now—in short order we are the best of friends, as though we had been traveling together for at least half a year. They really are delightful people, the two of them. When we part for the night, they have grown so dear to me that I feel genuine pleasure as I climb up to my bunk on the third tier directly beneath the roof of the second-class compartment, reflecting on how terribly I shall sweat now, simply to afford the gracious lady a pleasant night's rest!
