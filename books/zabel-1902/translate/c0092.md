### 3. How the Amur Basin Finally Became Russian.

The history of the Russification of Siberia reveals the remarkable fact that the Chinese border was an even greater obstacle to Russia’s advance than the Pacific Ocean. While Russian trappers, Cossacks, and merchants had long been ranging across the Bering Strait, annexing Alaska to the Russian domain, and in their forward roving had even taken possession of the more southerly coastal stretches of the Pacific Ocean down as far as California before the stream of European immigrants pouring across North America from east to west ever reached these regions, Russian policy endeavoured, without any notable success, to win back from China by the spoonful the advantages that had been surrendered by the bushel in the Treaty of Nerchinsk. Even though Peter the Great had still managed to wrest from his contemporary Kangxi by diplomatic means the concession that a Russian political agent might take up residence in Beijing—incidentally the first and, for 140 years, the last foreign envoy in the northern capital—the death of this last great ruler of China, which followed soon afterwards, brought in its train a xenophobic backlash that once again rendered illusory the advantage just gained by the Russian side. Highly characteristic of the manner in which the two powers negotiated with one another, moreover, was the ceremony attending the reception of the Russian negotiator, Captain Leon Izmaylov, by the Son of Heaven. Since the Russian envoy refused to perform the demanded kowtow, but without it would have been denied an audience with the Emperor and his mission would have remained hopeless, a compromise was reached: first, a high-ranking Chinese mandarin threw himself down before the Tsar’s letter about to be presented, and then Izmaylov performed his kowtow before the Emperor of China! The political agent left behind by him, de Lange, subsequently lived in his house like a prisoner and was eventually expelled from Beijing. The freedom of trade at the border established in the Treaty of Nerchinsk was likewise subjected to chicanery from the Chinese side.
