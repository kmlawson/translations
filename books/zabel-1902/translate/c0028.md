4. For the maintenance of order in the city, the Chief of Police (an officer) has been appointed, to whom an assistant and an adequate number of police officers are assigned. In order to maintain order on the river and among the junks, a guard ship is established under the command of an officer and an adequate number of harbor police. In addition, in order to establish thorough order in the city, the police are tasked with determining the number of inhabitants, houses, and public institutions. The harbor police are to prevent the arrival of junks carrying weapons, Chinese soldiers, and munitions of war.

5. The tax inspector and revenue officer, together with his staff, collects from the Chinese population the customary taxes and levies, as well as the taxes and levies imposed [[p63]] by the Civil Administrator. The tax inspector collects and disburses all funds, both government funds and taxes and levies, and is responsible for them. The accounts must be rendered every three months to the Commander-in-Chief of Kwantung Province.

6. The Chinese Maritime Customs Office shall come under the supreme supervision of the Imperial Russian Government, but will retain its present organization. The rights of the officials with respect to their service and salary remain unchanged. On the other hand, the Russian Government is at the same time responsible for the revenues of the customs office and will be represented in the customs service by a commissioner whose rights

[Illustration: Russian staging post in the Laoye Temple of Shanhaiguan (p. 35).]

are to be determined in a special official document. In the Chinese Maritime Customs Service there is a separate department for the collection of customs duties from Chinese junks and from the Chinese coastal crews; its revenues shall not be included in the maritime customs revenues, but shall be at the disposal of the Civil Governor. The head of this department will receive a salary supplement.

7. In order to decide legal cases between natives, a court shall be established in accordance with the principles of the mixed courts in China. Under its jurisdiction fall both police court matters and cases between Chinese and Russians or those foreigners who have no consular representation of their own on the spot. Disputes between foreigners who are sued by Chinese belong before the respective consular court; cases in which a Chinese is sued by a foreigner belong before [[p64]] the municipal court. Capital crimes committed by Chinese, as well as highway robbery, treason, or the smuggling of contraband of war, shall be tried before a court-martial composed of officers.

8. To exercise supreme supervision over the sanitary conditions of the city and to provide sanitary assistance to the inhabitants, a physician with the necessary auxiliary personnel has been appointed.

9. The heads of the various departments, namely the Chief of Police, the tax inspector and revenue officer, the municipal judge, and the sanitary commissioner, as well as the commissioner of the customs office, shall be appointed or dismissed by decree of the Commander-in-Chief of Kwantung Province and of the troops stationed therein, upon the proposal of the Civil Administrator.

10. The Civil Administrator possesses the right, in agreement with the individual department heads, to engage foreigners and Chinese for service in the various administrative departments and to determine their salaries.

11. The regulations for each individual administrative department are to be drafted by the Civil Administrator in conjunction with the heads of the various departments, and submitted to the Commander-in-Chief for approval.

12. For meeting the expenditures of the provisional civil administration and covering its costs, the Russian Government grants advances, which are to be covered by the taxes and revenues collected from the Chinese, as well as by the revenues of the Chinese department of the Maritime Customs Office.”

Thus far the official document, which is signed by the “Commander-in-Chief of Kwantung Province as well as its troops and of the naval forces in the Pacific,” Vice Admiral Alekseyev.

In addition, I would add that the revenues of the Maritime Customs Office, in so far as they derive from foreign vessels, are for the time being collected by the Russian Government and paid into the Russo-Chinese Bank, or as they say, “deposited.” Moreover, let it also be noted at once that the Russians, as representatives of the Chinese Government, also collect from Europeans the customary three percent ad val. fee on land sales, which was formerly paid to the Chinese Government as a levy for registering the purchase in the land registry. This regulation was published on December 19, and all land sales that have taken place since the time of the Russian occupation are deemed not legally valid unless the three percent has been paid in arrears.
