#### March 3.

In the morning, the 25th Infantry Division, stationed east of Shalingpu, and General Shatilov's column were attacked by the Japanese. The 25th Division fell back as far as Yuhuantun; fugitives reached Lanjiatun, which is southwest of the Mukden railway station.

Shatilov's division held out near Zhanggutun, but that village was evacuated that same day or the next. General Shatilov suffered a concussion in the fighting.

Following the dispatch of Topornin's and Shatilov's columns, *there was once again no general reserve available*. For lack of available troops, the army high command [[p308]] had already on March 1 withdrawn units from other formations; on March 3, of the troops of this kind that had arrived during the night, the following were at the Mukden railway station: *a composite division of the 17th Army Corps* (Infantry Regiments Nos. 12, 138, and 140 with artillery, plus three battalions of the 147th Samara [Regiment] from the 1st Army Corps!). On March 3, this new reserve was sent to Houchai on the Xinmintun road, so that on the evening of that day, the Russian western group stood in a fairly continuous line from the latter locality down to Erzhaizi on the right bank of the Hun River, opposing the direct attacks on Mukden.

Now, however, there would once again have been no general reserve available, had there not arrived on this day, marching in from the east shrouded in dust and exhausted, a column that then bivouacked south of Mukden on both sides of the railway: it was the 1st Siberian Corps! Having been ordered from the extreme right to the extreme left wing, scarcely had it arrived in Shihoucheng when it was ordered back again; and to ensure that here too organic unity was by no means preserved, one of its regiments had remained with the Rennenkampf group and one with the 3rd Corps, in which latter corps, besides the Vyborg Regiment, troops from the 6th and 1st Siberian Corps were now mingled! And the 1st Siberian Corps in particular would have needed to be spared; back when it was still under General Stackelberg's command, it had always been the corps called upon to pull the chestnuts out of the fire. At Sandepu alone it had lost 8,000 men, so that its total casualties since the beginning of the campaign amounted to *one hundred and twenty-five percent* of its original strength.

#### March 4 and 5.

Little fighting took place on March 4. On this day, attempts were made to reorganize the units, which had already become heavily intermingled. South of the Hun River, the 10th Army Corps and 5th Siberian Corps evacuated their positions of March 3. The heavy guns of the 10th Corps had already been sent away by rail on March 2.

On March 4, the Second Army command had issued an attack disposition [[p309]] for March 5, which, however, was never carried out. But this disposition of March 4 provides a rough overview of the grouping of the troops on the western wing on that day.

On March 4, General Gerschelmann was supposed to hold the Sujiapu sector with Infantry Regiments Nos. 33, 121, and 122, along with six foot and two mortar batteries; however, the army disposition already lists these regiments on that day on the right, northern bank of the Hun River, so holding Sujiapu had to be abandoned.

In summary, on the evening of March 4 south of the Hun River under General von der Launitz, we find the following belonging to the Second Army: the 5th Siberian [Corps] (from the Third Army) minus Infantry Regiments Nos. 215 and 241, but plus Infantry Regiment No. 34, which had engaged in an action near Yinerpu. Furthermore, those parts of the 8th Army Corps and the riflemen not mentioned in the army disposition for March 5 (Infantry Regiments Nos. 53, 54, 57, and 60, Rifle Regiment No. 6, and the 1st Brigade—excluding detachments?).

The army high command was already finding it difficult to keep track of the troop distribution, especially where the enemy was pressing, that is, on both sides of the Hun River.

North of the Hun River from Majiapu to Changxintun, the following were grouped for attack according to the disposition for March 5: Lieutenant General Tserpitsky (commander of the 10th Army Corps) with General Gerschelmann's group near Majiapu, then seven rifle regiments, further Infantry Regiment No. 56 (from the 8th Army Corps), Infantry Regiment No. 215 (from the 5th Siberian [Corps]); and adjoining them, the 25th Infantry Division in the Yuhuantun–Niuxintun area.

It is worth noting that at noon on March 4, Lieutenant General Tserpitsky appeared in the military attachés' dining car and declared that at that moment he had no troops whatever at his disposal, as they had all been reassigned away from him by the army high command! It was not until around 2:00 p.m. that he was summoned to the army high command, where he was entrusted with the command of the troops designated in the disposition for March 5.
