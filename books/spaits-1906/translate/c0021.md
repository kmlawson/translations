### General Concept for the Execution of the Advance.

*Terrain.* It is flat between Xifangtai and Yingkou.

Since the general direction of the roads located here runs west to east, whereas the detachment's direction of advance was north to south, almost the entire march proceeded cross-country over the deeply furrowed, ploughed gaoliang fields, which certainly had an adverse effect on the troops' performance.

The Liaohe, the Hunhe, and their tributaries were still frozen solid; in the vicinity of Yingkou, however, the crust of ice was already so weak that the crossing of the batteries required time-consuming preparations.

The country's wealth of resources is great. *I have never seen a country where every crumb of soil was more intensively utilized for agriculture than in Manchuria.*

The detachment moved through an area hitherto little touched by the war, which makes the provisioning precautions described above appear all the more superfluous.

*Direction of march.* In order to preserve the element of surprise, the Japanese left wing had to be widely outflanked,

[Illustration: Finnish ambulance wagons.] [[p79]] which necessitated crossing the Liaohe twice. To keep the movement concealed, it would have been more advantageous to carry it out entirely west of the Liaohe, but this was not possible, as the southern course of the river—which is already very wide and whose alluvial land is marshy—would have called regaining the left bank into question; thus, after turning the Japanese left wing, the march returned to the shortest route.

*Column formation.* In order to cross the river more quickly, the march was begun in four columns and then continued in three, which actually formed merely a marching checkerboard across the deployment frontage. The heads of the columns were about two versts apart, so that adjacent columns could see each other.

The center column, which General Mishchenko led, had the direction.

*Reconnaissance.* Just as I later had opportunity to observe for myself, here too the *sending out of far-ranging intelligence patrols* was omitted. At daybreak (between 6 and 7 a.m.), however, several officer patrols did go ahead, which as a rule were recalled towards evening.

*Security.* During the march, the center column provides security to the front, the flank columns to the flanks.

During the overnight bivouac, the security troops form a ring around the camp.

The strength of the security troops was one regiment per column; their relief was left to the column commanders.

*Overnight stay.* Bivouac in the intervals between the marching columns.
