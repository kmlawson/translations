Or with the childlike, naive hopes that the army then once again placed in the old General Linevich, who, after all, already as commander of the First Army, had known how to exploit the numerical superiority of troops at his disposal for nothing better than a lackluster defensive! More of all that later; for now, let us cast a glance at the events that unfolded from the beginning to the end of the battle on the western flank and in the center.

The relevant data I gathered after the battle in Harbin, or in part had recounted to me by eyewitnesses along the way. If the purely objective account of the fighting that now follows holds little appeal for the non-military reader, he may skip the next few pages and only rejoin me on my journey from Tieling to Harbin!

## The Fighting on the Western Flank and in the Center from 25 February to 9 March.¹)

### The Planned Resumption of the Offensive.

On 19 February, following a conference at Army Headquarters in Sujiatun, a renewed attack on Sandepu had been decided upon. The capture of this place was to mark the first stage of the general offensive upon which Kuropatkin had now resolved for the third time since the retreat from Liaoyang.

The initial measures for this were the reinforcement of the 1st Siberian Corps, which formed the reserve of the Second Army, by half of the 8th East Siberian Rifle Division.

The attack on Sandepu was to have begun with the *Rifle Corps* and the *8th Army Corps* on *25 February at 6:00 a.m.*

The 1st Siberian [Corps] had already marched south from Shakhezi. General Rennenkampf was to *advance southwestward* with the cavalry on the *25th*.

*The participation of the other two armies was again planned to be merely demonstrative for this day*—thus a repetition of the mistake responsible for the first failure at Sandepu!

This time, however, it never even came to that!

On the evening of the 24th, all these offensive movements were halted—*the attack on Sandepu was canceled!!*

From the extreme left flank, from the Qinghecheng Detachment, unfavorable telegraphic reports had arrived; Japanese forces advancing in the mountains had, as we already know, forced General Alekseyev with the Eastern Detachment to evacuate the positions pushed forward there toward the southeast, which created the impression at Army Headquarters of a major Japanese offensive that, with progressing success, could have as its objective the defile of Tieling in the rear of the Russian army.

Given all the vulnerability of their own line of communications to the north, the best security for it in this case would surely have consisted in vigorously carrying out the already planned and prepared attack on Sandepu, in which, of course, the Second and First Armies should not have looked on idly either!

For this, however, the Army High Command lacked the necessary energy! Always it was only concern for a secure retreat, and never the firm resolve to attack the enemy with all available forces!

*Thus, once again, at the enemy's first move, the initiative was conceded to him.*

If the commander lacked the will for decisive action on this day, his chief of staff demonstrated his complete operational ineptitude at the very same time: *The 1st Siberian Corps was halted on its march toward Sandepu and routed via Zhangsamutun to Shihuicheng, thus shifted from the right to the left flank of the battle front.* Sandepu–Shihuicheng, sixty kilometers as the crow flies! Likewise, the 72nd Division was pulled out of the fighting line from the 6th Army Corps (center), along with still other troops, in order to shift them eastwards—to the 3rd Corps!

Thus the eastern sector was significantly reinforced while the western flank and the center were weakened.

Lieutenant General Linevich, commander of the First Army, whose headquarters fell back further eastward from Huangshan to Shihuicheng on 26 February, had, after uniting the forces already under his command and those newly arrived [[p304]] (exclusive of the 4th and 2nd Siberian Corps), more than eighty battalions:

The expectation that our popular general would use this crushing numerical superiority for a counteroffensive remained unfulfilled; he allowed himself to be restricted by the enemy to the defensive, in which he also remained until the end. The good gentleman! He was, after all, the one who had once said: “The Japanese are *Asiatics*; we must take them by the forelock and teach them culture!” Now he would have had the opportunity!
