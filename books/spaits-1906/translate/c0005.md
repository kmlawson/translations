Russia, however, still had sufficient material at its disposal to dispatch another strong auxiliary squadron consisting of 7 battleships, 1 armored gunboat, 4 cruisers, 4 torpedo cruisers, 14 destroyers, 1 mine transport, and 2 hospital ships. Among these was, above all, the battleship *Slava*, a sister ship of the *Orel*, *Borodino*, *Alexander III*, and *Suvorov*, which formed the core of the Second Squadron. How little conviction there was here even in February regarding the necessity of dispatching a second squadron is shown by the fact that when the shipyard where the *Slava* was being built declared itself ready to complete the armored vessel by autumn, this offer was not even given the necessary attention. When, after two months, it was nonetheless found necessary to proceed more energetically with the *Slava*, the incident with the cruiser *Orel* occurred, whose bearings overheated during its first sea trial as a result of sand having been introduced, and which also sank shortly afterward in the harbor of Kronstadt. In order to replace certain damaged engine parts quickly, they were taken from the *Slava*, on which [[p21]] work was then suspended again. For the Third Squadron, the battleships *Imperator Nikolai I* and *Imperator Alexander II* were also available, though certainly not new (1887 and 1889). In Libau, moreover, three modern armored ships equipped with the latest artillery were fitting out, namely the *General-Admiral Apraksin*, *Admiral Ushakov*, and *Admiral Senyavin* (1893–1896). Furthermore, the old battleship *Pyotr Veliky* was also available. Of the cruisers, I would also mention the *Pamyat Azova*, *Vladimir Monomakh* (sister ship of the *Dmitry Donskoy*), and the modern *Admiral Kornilov*. Of the destroyers, six have been under construction for ten years. The firm of Krupp had, as is well known, presented the Russian government with a submarine valued at one and a half million rubles as a gift, and in return had also received an order for five more such boats. As a repair ship, the huge new *Okean*, and as hospital ships, the *Polyarnaya Zvezda* and *Standart* should be mentioned. Owing to their speed, the last three could also be used for reconnaissance, in which case, however, they would have to be placed in the ranks of combatant vessels.

The mission of the Third Squadron depends on the timing of its departure. The favorable outcome would undoubtedly be if it could still enter into action simultaneously with the Second Squadron. In this case, the older ships could cover the better ships of the combined squadrons. They could find a further use in hunting Japanese transport ships. If the two squadrons intervene in events at different times, the mission can only be accomplished if Rozhestvensky succeeds in first winning a partial success over Togo, joining up with the Vladivostok squadron, and postponing the decisive battle until the arrival of the Third Squadron. All speculation regarding the establishment of a base of operations, its location, Togo steaming out as far as Singapore to meet them, etc., is still decidedly premature today. Equally groundless are the rumors that Russia intends to send its Black Sea Fleet to the Far East.

[[p22]]

Apart from the fact that, after the Dogger Bank incident, Russia would hardly be inclined to bring new political trouble upon itself by forcing the Dardanelles, its warships have far too limited an operating radius for such an undertaking and, moreover, are fitted for oil-firing. After all, every day can bring new surprises that may completely alter the situation. One circumstance that had a disruptive effect on the dispatch of the Third Squadron was the shortage of marine engineers.

These were pretty much the data I gathered in Riga and later in Petersburg regarding Russia's naval strength at that time—or, expressed in the style of a reporter: obtained confidentially from a high-ranking personality at the Admiralty.
