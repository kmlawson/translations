### The Position of the Russian-Manchurian Army on 8 March.

The *First Army* under General of Infantry *Linevich* in the east, from Yingpan to Fushun; Lieutenant General *Rennenkampf*, with the Eastern Corps on the left wing of the First Army; the easternmost groups under Generals *Danilov* and *Maslov*; cavalry pushed forward into the southern side valleys of the Hunhe. Colonel *Madritov*, who had been detached toward the Korean border with several Cossack detachments and a *honghuzi* detachment in Russian pay, was likely already cut off from the army by this time.

The *Third Army* under Lieutenant General *Bilderling*. The direct protection of Mukden from the south fell to the Third Army; standing from east to west with the VI and XVII Army, V Siberian, and VIII Army Corps, and those parts of the Rifle Corps that had still remained south of the Hunhe, it extended from Fuling—the Mukden bridgehead, consisting of five large redoubts—to Machiapu, where it linked up with General *Herschelman's* group. In the army's rear, all terrain sectors had already been prepared in the autumn of last year as potential rallying positions.

The *Second Army* under General of Cavalry Baron *Kaulbars*, forming the western group and at the same time the focal point of events, even though the decisive action was not to take place here. Extending from General Herschelman's group northward from the Hunhe toward the Xinmintun road, there stood: General *Tserpitsky* at Yangshitun, the 25th Division under General *Topornin* at Yuhuantun, General *Gerngross* at Houha. For the protection of Mukden to the north, in the woods near the Imperial Tombs, stood General von der Launitz; subordinated to him were the 41st Brigade under the divisional commander General *Bürger*, which had now regained contact with the army, the [[p321]] 1st and 10th Rifle Regiments, and a composite regiment from the 4th Siberian Corps.

For the direct protection of the railway, the 2nd Dragoon and 1st Verkhneudinsk Cossack Regiments assembled on the afternoon of the 8th near the Mukden railway station. The wounded General Mishchenko addressed these troops, after which the brigade rode northward along the railway.

The *Army High Command was in Mukden.*

The Army High Command had in no way—at least noticeably—exerted its influence on the commander of the First Army, General of Infantry *Linevich*, to induce him to exploit the numerical superiority placed at his disposal against the enemy for an *energetic offensive*; it may therefore well be said that—far removed from any thought of an offensive—even the resistance at Mukden amounted merely to a fight to gain time.

On the 8th, stubborn fighting was already taking place to the *north of Mukden*; south of the Hunhe, the Japanese pressed hard behind the columns withdrawing to the Hunhe line.

It seems as though this critical situation was required to awaken the energy of the commander-in-chief.

Now, as the enemy's iron ring from the west, south, and east closes tighter from hour to hour, Adjutant General *Kuropatkin* arrives at a resolute—yet *belated*—decision.
